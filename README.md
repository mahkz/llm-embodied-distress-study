# When the Body Speaks First

**How LLM chatbots respond to early bodily-emotional distress in English and Persian. An exploratory study.**

Mahsa Kazemzadeh, independent researcher, Edinburgh, UK (October 2026)

> **Content note:** the model responses in this repository mention health emergencies, and some mention suicide or self-harm.

## Summary

People often notice distress in their body before they can name a feeling, for example a heavy heart, a knot in the stomach or a tight chest. This study asked how three widely used LLMs read such messages, and whether this differs between English and Persian.

- 17 short messages (8 scenarios in English and Persian, plus one extra English wording) were each sent 3 times to 3 models, giving 153 responses.
- A probe added 16 body drawings made by 4 adults (48 responses), a neutral-question condition (48 responses) and body maps produced by the models themselves (162 outputs).

Main findings:

- Figurative bodily language was often read as a possible emergency in English ("my heart has felt heavy": 5 of 9), never in Persian («دلم گرفته»: 0 of 9).
- The models moved from feeling to body, but not from body to feeling. They placed sadness in the throat and chest when asked (18 of 18), yet recognised sadness in none of 12 drawings of it.
- The gap appears to be one of design rather than capability.

The full report is in [`report/report.pdf`](report/report.pdf).

## How the coding was done

This is stated openly because LLMs were involved in coding.

- **Text responses (balanced sample of 51):** the author coded 28. For the other 23, an LLM (Claude) proposed codes using the same codebook and examples the author had coded, and the author reviewed and corrected all of them. For 10 of these, the author coded interpretation (C1) herself. The `coded_by` column in `coding/reference_codes.csv` shows this for each row.
- **Second coder:** DeepSeek coded the responses independently with the same codebook. 110 of 153 rows were coded before the run stopped. `code/reliability.py` reproduces the agreement values in the report (49 overlapping responses).
- **Drawing responses (48):** Claude proposed codes, calibrated on examples the author had coded, and the author reviewed and corrected all 48. The regions marked in the drawings were coded by the author.

## Running the code

```bash
pip install -r code/requirements.txt
export OPENROUTER_API_KEY="your-key"   # never commit your key
python code/collect.py
```

The API key is read only from the environment variable. Model outputs will differ from the published data, because the models are non-deterministic and change over time.

## Ethics and consent

This was an independent study and did not go through an institutional ethics committee. No personal data were sent to the models. All four drawers gave written consent for their drawings to be sent to the chatbots and to be published without their names. Pilot drawings are not included.

## Limitations

Small samples, single-turn messages, one main coder with LLM assistance, and no data on how people experience the responses. See Section 6 of the report.
